<p style="font-size: 18px;">“Ship operating companies often hit a spot in their growth journey&nbsp;when they&nbsp;struggle to track where money is going, how to make&nbsp;cost control&nbsp;traceable,&nbsp;and forecasting&nbsp;always&nbsp;seems&nbsp;difficult.”</p>



<p style="font-size: 18px;">Sound familiar? </p>



<p style="font-size: 18px;">When different departments follow their own practices and use siloed software solutions, it leads to inconsistency, errors, and difficulty enforcing procurement policies. Transactions happen outside approved systems, organizations struggle to track where money is going, and cost control&nbsp;is reduced to a tedious deluge of emails and phone calls. Reliable&nbsp;forecasting&nbsp;is often limited&nbsp;by&nbsp;different sources&nbsp;of truth: heavy reliance on emails, spreadsheets, and paper invoices increases cycle times, raises processing costs, and&nbsp;swallow momentum. </p>



<p style="font-size: 18px;">So how should fleet managers tackle the rapid rise in volume without compromising operational efficiency?&nbsp;</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Enter <strong>the Magic Wand</strong></h2>



<p></p>



<div class="wp-block-columns is-layout-flex wp-container-core-columns-is-layout-28f84493 wp-block-columns-is-layout-flex">
<div class="wp-block-column is-layout-flow wp-block-column-is-layout-flow">
<p>The magic wand seems to be Procure-to-Pay (P2P, Purchase-to-Pay, Self-Billing), the end-to-end business process that covers how an organization requests, purchases, receives, pays for, and records goods or services. It typically starts with a purchase requisition, moves through supplier selection and purchase order creation, and continues with receiving goods, invoice processing, and payment. The goal of P2P is to ensure spend control, operational efficiency, and financial accuracy while maintaining strong supplier and customer relationships. By standardizing and automating these steps, shipping networks can reduce costs, improve compliance with procurement policies, gain visibility into spending, improve the quality of invoices, and shorten payment cycles. </p>
</div>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>The Magic Wand&nbsp;Applied to Shipping Operations</strong>&nbsp;</h2>



<p>However, integrating&nbsp;procure-to-pay into the operations of a shipping line relies heavily on&nbsp;aligning procurement and finance processes with the realities of fleet operations, port activities, and global supplier networks.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>



<p>As shipping lines have decentralized, asset‑heavy operations, their&nbsp;P2P should be designed around&nbsp;<strong>events</strong>&nbsp;in&nbsp;key operational areas&nbsp;like&nbsp;<em>fleet&nbsp;operations&nbsp;</em>by looking into the procurement of fuels, spare parts, lubricants, safety equipment, and maintenance services&nbsp;<em>and</em>&nbsp;be closely&nbsp;linked to vessel schedules and planned maintenance systems.&nbsp;When it comes to&nbsp;<em>port and&nbsp;terminal&nbsp;operations</em>,&nbsp;port services such as stevedoring, pilotage, towage, port fees, and waste disposal should flow through standardized service orders,&nbsp;which are&nbsp;tied to&nbsp;the specific&nbsp;port call.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>



<p>Effective P2P integration depends on connection with a shipping company’s planned maintenance system, fleet management system, voyage planning system, and the ERP / finance systems.  </p>



<p>Integrating P2P into operations enables&nbsp;spend tracking by vessel, voyage, port, and cost category, identification of cost variances (e.g., bunker prices, port fees)&nbsp;and better budgeting and forecasting tied directly to operational activity.&nbsp;This is critical for margin management in&nbsp;today’s&nbsp;volatile shipping market.&nbsp;  </p>



<p style="font-size: 18px;"><strong>An embedded P2P can especially facilitate smart financial operations around a port-call.  </strong></p>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="1">
<li>Early demand capture enables spend control before the vessel&nbsp;arrives, and&nbsp;PO-backed services reduce disputes and support invoice matching.&nbsp;</li>
</ol>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="2">
<li>Service delivery execution,&nbsp;operational confirmation,&nbsp;possible emergency&nbsp;buys&nbsp;– in all these fields&nbsp;P2P supports&nbsp;an overview of&nbsp;controlled exceptions without blocking operations.&nbsp;</li>
</ol>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="3">
<li>Accurate consumption data improves accounting and cost&nbsp;allocation;&nbsp;&nbsp;</li>
</ol>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="4">
<li>Clean service confirmation reduces invoice delays,&nbsp;&nbsp;</li>
</ol>



<ol class="wp-block-list" start="5">
<li>Automated matching&nbsp;invoices&nbsp;minimize disputes and manual&nbsp;rework.&nbsp;A&nbsp;timely&nbsp;payment, based on matched and exception-cleared invoices,&nbsp;strengthens agent and port supplier relationships.&nbsp;&nbsp;</li>
</ol>



<p>An integrated Purchase-to-Pay improves cost predictability, reduces post‑voyage disputes, enables real‑time voyage P&amp;L&nbsp;and balances&nbsp;the&nbsp;speed of operations with financial control.&nbsp;</p>



<p style="font-size: 16px;">For a shipping line, P2P works best when it is embedded into daily operations, tightly integrated with fleet and port systems, and flexible enough to support global, fast-moving activities. Done right, it improves cost control, operational efficiency, compliance, and supplier performance—without slowing the ships down.&nbsp;The smartest way of embedding P2P for a shipping line is to have it as a part of the ERP system, and this is exactly what&nbsp;Kaleris&nbsp;Odyssey&nbsp;provides.&nbsp;</p>
